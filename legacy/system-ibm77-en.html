IBM PS/2 Model 77 486
The IBM PS/2 Model 77 belongs to the professional PS/2 family and was announced in October 1992. It combines the 486 generation with IBM's 32-bit Micro Channel architecture, integrated SCSI and XGA-2 graphics, positioning it as a high-end business and workstation platform.

TECHNICAL DATA
Introduction1992
Machine Type9577
CPUIntel 80486SX 33 MHz or 80486DX2 66 MHz
Bus32-bit Micro Channel (MCA), 5 slots
RAM8 MB standard, up to 32 MB on the system board
GraphicsXGA-2, 1 MB video memory
StorageSCSI; typical models with 212 MB or 400 MB hard disks
Floppy3.5-inch, 2.88 MB media-sense
Operating systemsIBM PC DOS 5.x and OS/2 2.x; classic Windows environments are also important for our tests

PERIOD CONFIGURATIONS
The original 9577 family was offered with configurations including 486SX/33 and 486DX2/66 processors with 8 MB RAM. IBM paired these systems with XGA-2, SCSI hard disks and 2.88 MB floppy drives.

Later Model 77i/77s families from 1994 could also offer DX4/100 processors, additional cache, IDE or SCSI and up to 64 MB RAM. Those later machines belong to the extended Model 77 family rather than the original 1992 configuration.

TYPICAL USE CASES
Business PC and workstation
MS-DOS and Windows 3.x
OS/2 workstation
Network and office applications
CAD / technical software
Corporate environments using MCA and SCSI expansion
